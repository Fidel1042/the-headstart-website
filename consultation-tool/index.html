Sales call · 6 stages · 5 questions, then the test only if you need it

1

Open

Warm them up, then set the agenda. About one minute.

Say

"Hey [name], how are you? Whereabouts are you based?"

"I'll ask you a few things so I can work out where you're at and match you with the right mentor. Then I'll take anything you want to ask me. Sound good?"

Get the little "yeah" before you start asking. That's your permission.

Do not describe the service here. Do not tell your own story yet.

2

Diagnose: the 5 questions

They talk, you listen. Take notes, do not pitch. This is most of the call.

1 · Industry

"What industry are you trying to get into?"

If vague, probe once: "Any particular function? Analyst, consulting, marketing?" Vagueness here is itself the diagnosis, and it's a signal to run the test later.

2 · Free help so far, the big one

"What have you tried so far in terms of getting help? Not applications, help. Career centre, networking events, societies, anyone giving you advice?"

"And what came of it? Did it change anything?"

"Why do you think it didn't work for you?"

They have to say it out loud. Thinking it is not the same. Do not answer for them and do not rescue the silence.

If they say "I don't know why it didn't work"

"What did they actually tell you to do?" then "And who gave you that advice?"

"That person has never sat the grad interview you're sitting, or they sat it fifteen years ago in a different market. They can tell you what a good candidate looks like from across the table. They can't tell you what's specifically wrong with you."

Two points, then stop and let them agree: the advice wasn't personalised, and it wasn't relevant. "Yeah, that's exactly it" is the micro-yes that makes the rest of the call easy.

3 · Timeline

"When do you need this job by? And where are you at with study and your visa?"

If vague: "Grad applications for a February start are open now and most close around November. So to start next year, you need to be interviewing in the next couple of months."

Get a date, not a vibe. This is your lever on every delay objection later.

4 · Barrier, this decides the test

"What would you say is your biggest barrier right now?"

Softer version if they've been guarded: "How are you feeling about the whole thing at the moment?"

Barrier version for someone already venting, feelings version for someone clipped and factual.

5 · Type of mentoring

"What kind of mentoring do you actually need? Someone hands-on and tactical who'll sit there and work through the material with you, or someone more advice-based who points you in the right direction and you run with it?"

This presupposes the purchase, and it's the real brief for the mentor. Log the answer. Tactical means the work happens inside the session with no homework.

If they say "same industry" or "someone who was international"

"That's the bare minimum, that's what the guarantee covers. Everyone you'd get has worked in your industry and landed a role here as an international student. I'm asking beyond that."

If they fish for tactics, "what did you do to get your offers?"

"I'll show you exactly, but first I want to get your full picture." Do not give the playbook away for free here.

After each question, go quiet. Let them fill the silence.

3
Only if you need it

The test

A lever, not a step. Some people already know they need help. Making them perform wastes three minutes and can sour a warm buyer.

Pull it when
  • They can't name a real barrier
  • They blame the market, luck, or not applying enough
  • They frame it as a small fix, "just need my resume tidied"
  • They were vague on industry
  • They go soft at the close, "let me think about it"
Leave it holstered when
  • They named a specific painful gap already
  • They're asking how soon they can start
  • They've been rejected at interview or assessment centre and know it
  • They're visibly ready to buy
Say, ask permission first

"Can I try something quick with you? It takes two minutes and it'll tell me more than anything else I could ask."

"I'll give you a minute to prep, plan it however you want, then answer it like it's the real thing: tell me a little bit about yourself."

Give the real minute and stay silent through it. The prep window does the work, and it makes the failure fair so they can't say "I wasn't prepared".

Then give no feedback. Hardest rule on the call

"Okay. I understand where you're at."

Move straight on, no pause. A pause invites "wait, what did I do wrong?" The critique is the paid product.

If they ask for feedback on their answer

"That's exactly what your mentor takes you through properly. I just needed to see where you're starting from."

Never coach here. Never run this on someone who is already sold.

4

Verdict, then pitch when asked

Mirror them back, name the gap, then hand over: "That's everything from me. What have you got for me?"

Say, mirror first

"So you want to get into [industry], right now you're [current situation], and the thing in the way is [their barrier]."

"You're competing through the front door everyone uses. That's almost never an effort problem, and it's fixable."

If they ask "how does this work?" open Session structure

Name their quadrant, walk the stages, and name the one that gets the weight because it's their gap. "For you we camp on [stage]." Then: "Does that path make sense?"

Never say you skip a stage. Weight, not removal.

If they ask "how would the mentor help me?" mirror question 2 back

"You said the advice you got was generic and you never found out what was actually wrong. That's the whole job. Your mentor sits with your interview answers, your resume, your LinkedIn, and tells you exactly what to change, before and after, based on what hiring managers in that industry actually screen for and what your strengths are."

Proof anchor, every call, company number first

"We started in February and have already placed 12 students into internships and grad roles, six of them international like you. Koko and I were international students stuck for months too, that's why we get it."

Then the mentor, one name, full conviction

"The person for you is [mentor]. [One-line story tied to their field]. Especially strong on [their gap]."

If "does networking actually work?"

"It's how I got my first two offers. Around half of roles go through referrals, not the front door."

If they have big or unrealistic aims, or argue back (e.g. "only top-tier")

Hear them out fully first. Then: "I hear you. Here's the realistic way in..." Never half-disqualify them on the spot.

Never pitch before they ask. Never offer two mentors. Never say "I'm torn."

5

Price

Anchor the session count first, then state the number flat and stop talking.

Say, count before price, always

"For someone in your spot, most people take around 12 to 15 sessions to land a role."

"It's $55 a session, only charged after each one, no lock-in."

Then go silent. Let them react first.

If "why not $35, the website showed $35?"

"That was our launch price in February. Since then we've placed 12 students, brought on mentors, and built the resources and tracking that come with every session. The price reflects what's in it now."

If money worry, or "it's a lot"

"12 sessions is about $660 to land a role paying $70k plus. And you only pay per session, as you go." Open the ROI tab and run their numbers.

Never say "we charge reasonably." Never walk the number down.

6

Close

Get the commitment on the call.

Say, authority first, then close

"Quick one, are you the one who decides on this, or is it a chat with your parents?"

"Any reason we couldn't get you started with [mentor] next week?"

Then stay silent. Do not fill it with more value.

If "let me think" or "can I tell you next week?"

"How about tomorrow?" Then: "Is this something you're looking to sort now, or still weighing up? What's making you want to wait, the money, the time, or whether it'll actually work?" Then handle the real one.

If they go soft and you never ran the test

This is the signal. Run it now (stage 3), then re-close off the back of it: "Based on what I just heard, waiting doesn't put you in a better spot."

If it's time or busyness

Use their date from question 3. "You're not close to ready, and that's fine, that's what this is for. But the mentor gives you the answers, you're the one executing them, and that takes reps. Waiting just means more sessions to get to the same place." Then the visa frame: "Plenty of things can wait. With a two-year visa, this one has a hard deadline on it."

Only if they ask "why is it paid?"

"We started free and people didn't do the work, because nothing was on the line." Never volunteer this. It spotlights the price and cheapens the value.

If parents pay

"Love that. Let me give you what you need to make it an easy yes for them. I'll send the parent resources." Set a firm deadline.

If buying signal, "when can I start?"

Stop selling. "Great, I'll send the agreement now, takes 30 seconds, and I'll lock [mentor] in for you."

Logistics

Send the agreement link plus card details. Open a WhatsApp group with the mentor. Set the first session time.

Never cave when they pause. Never joke about scams or trust.
