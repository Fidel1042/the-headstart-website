Sales call · 6 stages · questions, objections, what to say

1

Open

Warm them up, then set the agenda. About one minute.

Say

"Hey [name], how are you? Whereabouts are you based?"

"I'll ask you a few things to understand where you're at, then show you exactly how we'd help. Sound good?"

Get the little "yeah" before you start asking. That's your permission.

2

Diagnose

Understand their goal, situation and pain. They talk, you listen. Take notes, do not pitch yet.

Ask, in order

"What are you aiming for, role and industry?"

"Where are you at with study and your visa?"

"What have you tried so far, and what actually happens when you apply?"

"How are you feeling about it all right now?"

After each question, go quiet. Let them fill the silence.

If they fish for tactics — "what did you do to get your offers?"

"I'll show you exactly, but first I want to get your full picture." Do not give the playbook away for free here.

3

Verdict

Make them feel understood, then give one honest diagnosis. Understand fully before you challenge anything.

Say — mirror first

"So I see you want to get into [industry], right now you're [current situation], and you're feeling [how they feel]."

Then name the gap

"Here's what I think is really going on. You're competing through the front door everyone uses. That's almost never an effort problem. The gap is [their barrier], and it's fixable."

If they have big or unrealistic aims, or argue back (e.g. "only top-tier")

Hear them out fully first. Then: "I hear you. Here's the realistic way in..." Never half-disqualify them on the spot.

4

Pitch — proof, then mentor

This is exactly what we fix, and here is who fixes it.

Say — bridge and proof

"That's exactly what we fix. We started in February and have already placed 12 students into internships and grad roles, six of them international like you. Koko and I were international students stuck for months too, that's why we get it."

Then the mentor — one name, full conviction

"The person for you is [mentor]. [One-line story tied to their field]. He's especially strong on [their gap]."

"Most people in your spot take around 12 to 15 sessions to land a role."

If "does networking actually work?"

"It's how I got my first two offers. Around half of roles go through referrals, not the front door."

Never offer two mentors. Never say "I'm torn."

5

Price

State it flat and confident, then stop talking.

Say

"It's $55 a session, only charged after each one, no lock-in."

Then go silent. Let them react first.

If "why not $35, the website showed $35?"

"That was our launch price in February. Since then we've placed 12 students, brought on mentors, and built the resources and tracking that come with every session. The price reflects what's in it now."

If money worry, or "it's a lot"

"12 sessions is about $660 to land a role paying $70k plus. And you only pay per session, as you go." Open the ROI tab and run their numbers.

Never say "we charge reasonably." Never walk the number down.

6

Close

Get the commitment on the call.

Say — authority first, then close

"Quick one, are you the one who decides on this, or is it a chat with your parents?"

"Any reason we couldn't get you started with [mentor] next week?"

Then stay silent. Do not fill it with more value.

If "let me think"

"Totally fair. Is it the price, the timing, or are you unsure it'll work?" Then handle the real one.

If parents pay

"Love that. Let me give you what you need to make it an easy yes for them. I'll send the parent resources." Set a firm deadline.

If buying signal — "when can I start?"

Stop selling. "Great, I'll send the agreement now, takes 30 seconds, and I'll lock [mentor] in for you."

Logistics

Send the agreement link plus card details. Open a WhatsApp group with the mentor. Set the first session time.

Never cave when they pause. Never joke about scams or trust.
