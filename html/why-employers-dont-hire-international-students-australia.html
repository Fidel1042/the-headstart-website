Why Employers Don't Hire International Students in Australia Your Visa Is Only Half the Reason.

14 September 2026

A Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485) gives you 2 to 3 years of full working rights in Australia. No sponsorship. No hour limit. No restriction on industry. You pay the fee, which is AUD 5,750 for the primary applicant as of 1 July 2026. The employer pays nothing and signs nothing.

I blamed my visa for my rejections anyway. So has almost every international student I have mentored since.

Part of that blame is fair. A real slice of the Australian graduate market is closed to you. The rest of it is doing damage, because it keeps you applying to the wrong half of the market and explaining yourself badly in the half that would have hired you.

Do employers in Australia hire international students?

Between late May and late July 2026 we tracked 125 graduate roles, internships and vacationer programs open to international students and sent every one of them out through our weekly job alerts.

Technology led with 37 roles. Data and analytics had 22, accounting 21, finance 20. Sydney held 72 of the 125, Melbourne 37, Brisbane 19.

Those were live roles in one 9-week window, open to students on a student visa or a 485. The full breakdown by company and city is here: Companies Hiring International Students in Australia: 125 Roles We Tracked Last Quarter.

The part that is genuinely closed

A graduate program recruiter told us roughly 80% of Australian graduate programs lean toward citizens and permanent residents, even when the job ad never says it.

Defence, government and anything requiring a security clearance is a hard no. Some of the large structured graduate programs are a quiet no. You can spend six months applying into that group and never see a single first-round interview, and none of it will be about the quality of your application.

That is the honest half. The market is not fair and pretending otherwise helps nobody.

What follows from it is a filtering problem, not a reason to stop. If a meaningful share of grad programs is closed, every hour you spend applying into that share is an hour you did not spend on the share that is open.

What your 485 actually tells a recruiter

Most recruiters are not rejecting a visa subclass. They are asking one question: how long can this person stay, and do I have to do paperwork.

The 485 answers both well. A bachelor or coursework masters graduate gets 2 years. A research masters or PhD gets 3. Hong Kong and BNO passport holders get 5. Indian nationals with a first-class honours STEM bachelor or a masters get 3 under the trade deal.

Two years of unrestricted work is a longer runway than a working holiday maker, and it costs the employer nothing to use. Almost no student says this out loud in an application.

One line on your resume and LinkedIn does the work: "Full working rights in Australia until March 2029." State it once. Do not explain your PR plan. Long explanations invite doubt that was not there a minute ago.

The rejections that have nothing to do with your visa

I sent 173 applications before I got anywhere. When I finally got four offers, my visa had not changed. My process had.

Three things were doing the damage, and I see the same three in almost every student I speak to:

Volume instead of fit. Sixty Easy Apply submissions in a weekend feels like work and produces nothing. More on why that fails here: Why Job Search Is Not a Numbers Game.

Experience that does not line up with the role. Your retail shifts, your society exec role and your group project all count, but only if the resume connects them to what the job asks for.

Interview answers built for a different country. Fluent English is not the same as answering the way an Australian interviewer expects. This is where most international students lose offers they were qualified for.

None of those get fixed by permanent residency.

What to do in your first 6 months on a 485

Read the work rights line on the ad before you self-reject. "Australian citizenship required" is a wall. Silence is not a wall, and most ads are silent.

Weight your applications toward internships and vacationer programs. Of the 125 roles we tracked, 80 were internships or vacationer placements against 42 graduate programs, and firms regularly convert interns before the graduate round opens publicly.

Go where international students have already been hired. If a team has taken one, the objection has already been answered internally.

Apply within 6 months of finishing your course, and note that the 485 has an age limit of 35 at application for most streams. Delaying a second masters or a gap year can close the door entirely.

The choice underneath all of this

You can be right about the market. A large share of the graduate programs you are looking at were never available to you, and that is worth being angry about for an afternoon.

Or you can accept that it is going to be harder for you than for the person sitting next to you in that lecture, and spend your energy on the part of the market that will say yes.

Students on the same visa restrictions are getting offers in every field, every intake. They are not luckier. They are aiming at a smaller target on purpose, and they stopped arguing with the part of the market that was never going to move.
