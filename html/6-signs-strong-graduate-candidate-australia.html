6 Signs of a Strong Graduate Candidate in Australia (You Need at Least 3)

5 August 2026

After 500+ conversations with international students and 30+ offers secured across Tech, Business, and Engineering, I have identified 6 features that show up on the resumes of every strong graduate candidate in Australia.

You do not need all 6. You need at least 3 to stand a real chance at landing a graduate role.

Here they are.

1. Relevant internship experience

The most valuable thing on your resume. This is the "juice" that makes recruiters take you seriously.

Local Australian experience is preferred, but an internship from your home country also works. What matters is that a hirer can see you have done something similar to the role you are applying for. That builds their confidence you can do the job here.

One important note: virtual internships (like Forage) do not count as valid internship experience to most Australian hirers. They are useful for learning what a role looks like day to day, but they will not carry weight on your resume the same way a real placement does.

2. Society executive role

Being an executive means leading a function of a society, sometimes managing sub-committees underneath you. Not "member." Not "helped organise the event." Actually leading something.

This signals real-world experience that is distinct from an internship. Internships show you can do work inside a company. Executive roles show you can take responsibility for outcomes when nobody is telling you what to do. Recruiters treat the two as different signals, so having both is stronger than having twice as much of either.

3. 85+ WAM, a professional certificate, or wins in case competitions

Grades only matter above 85. If your WAM is 82, listing it is unlikely to help. If your WAM is 87, it becomes a real differentiator.

Below 85, there are still two ways to signal academic strength. A widely recognised professional certificate (CFA for Finance, CISSP for Cyber Security, and similar) makes a difference in industries that respect them. And case competition wins count if you placed in the top 3 or something similarly notable. "Participated" does not count.

4. A relevant passion project with real impact

Passion projects show recruiters you are proactive and genuinely interested in the field. But most passion projects on resumes are hollow. They exist so the candidate can say they have one.

To count, a passion project must have direct, preferably quantifiable, impact. On your daily life, on your community, or on other people. AI can help you execute a project faster than ever, but the impact part is non-negotiable. Building a website for yourself that nobody uses is not a passion project. Building a small tool that 30 classmates use every week is.

5. A unique personal story or memorable fun facts

Everyone has unique stories. Not everyone knows how to use them.

When a recruiter goes through hundreds of applications in a week, most candidates blur together. One or two well-chosen fun facts about yourself, tied loosely to the role, can make you stand out immediately. This is what interviewers remember when they sit down at the end of the day to debrief.

6. Australian job experience (casual or volunteering counts)

Local experience is king. It shows an employer you can communicate with people in Australia, which is one of the quiet reasons international students get filtered out at final rounds.

You do not need a corporate role for this to count. Casual or part-time work in retail, hospitality, or admin all count. If you cannot find paid work, consistent volunteering does the same job, as long as it is genuinely consistent (once every two weeks or more, not a one-off).

If you have at least 3, you have a real chance

If you tick 3 or more of these 6, you have a solid foundation. Your resume passes the first filter, your interviews have material to work with, and you are competing on roughly the same footing as the strongest candidates in your cohort.

If you have fewer than 3, you are not out of the running. Points 4, 5, and 6 are all achievable within 1 to 2 months of focused work. A passion project with real impact, one or two memorable personal fun facts, and casual local Australian experience or consistent volunteering are all things you can build inside a semester.

The candidates who go from weak to competitive in a few months are the ones who identify which of these 6 they are missing and put the work in on the ones that are actually addressable. That is what most students never do, and it is why most graduate applications get automated rejections.
